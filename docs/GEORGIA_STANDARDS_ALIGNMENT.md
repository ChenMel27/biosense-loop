# Georgia Standards Alignment: Cellular Respiration Pilot

## Authoritative target

The current Georgia CASE framework identifies the active science framework as **Science — Georgia Standards of Excellence**. The pilot aligns to:

- **S7L4:** examine the interdependence of organisms with one another and their environments.
- **S7L4.b:** develop a model describing matter cycling and energy flow among biotic and abiotic ecosystem components.

Official sources:

- [Georgia Standards of Excellence for Science in CASE](https://case.georgiastandards.org/27a08dc6-416e-11e7-ba71-02bd89fdd987/27a08dc6-416e-11e7-ba71-02bd89fdd987)
- [GaDOE Science GSE Frequently Asked Questions and Clarifications](https://inspire.gadoe.org/user-files/9391da0f-5dd6-4e64-8075-9bab16f9cf44.pdf)

The GaDOE clarification for S7L4.b says the emphasis is tracing matter movement and energy flow, not the biochemical mechanisms of photosynthesis and cellular respiration. It further explains that seventh graders should reason about roles of organisms and the environment, such as producers and consumers, rather than memorize the detailed steps of biogeochemical processes.

## Product interpretation

Cellular respiration is the **phenomenon used to reveal S7L4.b reasoning**, not a license to move the activity to a high-school biochemistry level.

| S7L4.b expectation | Student evidence elicited by BioSense | Excluded from the pilot |
|---|---|---|
| Develop and use a model | Written causal model linking organism, food matter, gases, energy, and environment | Reciting a definition or equation without a system relationship |
| Trace matter | Account for carbon/food matter as it is rearranged and moves among organism, air, soil, and other organisms | Molecular bookkeeping or balanced equations |
| Trace energy | Describe energy transfer/use and the eventual loss of some energy as heat | ATP yield calculations and pathway energetics |
| Connect biotic and abiotic components | Link organisms with air, soil, food, carbon dioxide, producers, consumers, or decomposers | Treating respiration as an isolated organelle fact |
| Stay within the grade boundary | Explain the conceptual role of cellular respiration | Glycolysis, Krebs cycle, electron-transport chain, chemiosmosis, or memorized stages |

## Learning objective

> Students will develop a conceptual model that traces matter separately from energy through a simple ecosystem and explains how cellular respiration connects organisms to biotic and abiotic components.

## Success evidence

A standards-aligned response should demonstrate all or most of the following without requiring advanced vocabulary:

1. Food contains matter that is rearranged rather than converted into energy or destroyed.
2. Cellular respiration in cells releases usable energy from food.
3. Matter can move to carbon dioxide, organism structures, waste, air, soil, or other organisms.
4. Energy flows through organisms and some eventually leaves the ecosystem as heat.
5. Cellular respiration occurs in plants and animals; breathing and cellular respiration are related but not identical.

## Review gate

Before classroom use, the partner teacher must confirm that:

- S7L4.b is the intended classroom standard and students have received prerequisite instruction;
- the prompt wording matches the local lesson sequence;
- the response expectations do not exceed the course scope; and
- accommodations and read-aloud language preserve the same construct.
