# Georgia Standards Alignment: Trait-Inheritance Pilot

## Governing standard

The pilot is aligned only to Georgia Standards of Excellence **S7L3.a**:

> Construct an explanation supported with scientific evidence of the role of genes and chromosomes in the process of inheriting a specific trait.

The official Georgia Department of Education clarification places this element under S7L3, the transfer of genetic information to traits. It treats S7L3.b as a separate expectation about comparing sexual and asexual reproduction. It also notes that memorizing the stages of meiosis or mitosis is not the key expectation at this grade level.

Official source: [Georgia Standards of Excellence Science Clarifications](https://inspire.gadoe.org/user-files/9391da0f-5dd6-4e64-8075-9bab16f9cf44.pdf)

## Assessment claim

> Given a short evidence card about one fictional organism and one trait, students will construct an explanation that connects a trait-related gene to its location on a chromosome, traces one relevant gene version from each parent, and uses supplied evidence to connect the inherited pair with the offspring's observed trait.

The activity operationalizes the three parts of the standard:

| S7L3.a requirement | Evidence elicited in ExitLoop | Human-scored dimension |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Construct an explanation | A 2–4 sentence claim–evidence–reasoning response | Evidence-linked explanation |
| Use scientific evidence | At least two facts from the fixed evidence card | Evidence selection and linkage |
| Explain roles of genes and chromosomes | Gene as trait-related inherited information; chromosome as the structure carrying the gene version | Gene–trait relationship and gene–chromosome relationship |
| Explain inheritance of a specific trait | One relevant chromosome/gene version from each parent linked to the offspring's bristle or seed-coat trait | Both-parent contribution and trait conclusion |

## Content boundaries

Included:

- genes as inherited information relevant to a specific trait;
- genes located at positions on chromosomes;
- a relevant chromosome and gene version contributed by each parent;
- evidence connecting an inherited gene-version pair to an observed trait;
- fictional single-gene cases used as simplified models for explanation.

Explicitly excluded:

- ecology, matter cycling, or energy flow;
- Punnett-square construction and probability calculations;
- memorization of meiosis or mitosis stages;
- DNA replication, transcription, translation, or protein-synthesis mechanisms;
- polygenic, sex-linked, or environmentally complex human traits;
- claims that a simple model represents all biological inheritance.

The B/b and C/c evidence cards provide the observations students need; students are not expected to infer dominance rules or calculate offspring ratios.

## Frozen content evidence

Initial phenomenon: fictional beetle bristle shape, with a marked gene location on chromosome 3, one gene version from each parent, the offspring's b/b evidence, and the observed curved-bristle relationship.

Near-transfer phenomenon: fictional plant seed-coat pattern, with a marked gene location on chromosome 2, one gene version from each parent, the seed's c/c evidence, and the observed spotted-coat relationship.

Fictional organisms reduce the risk of oversimplifying or stigmatizing human traits while preserving the inheritance reasoning required by S7L3.a.

## Approval gate

Before classroom use, the biology teacher must confirm that:

- S7L3.a is the intended learning objective;
- students have received sufficient prerequisite instruction about genes and chromosomes;
- the evidence-card language is age-appropriate and scientifically acceptable;
- the fictional single-gene model is described as a simplified case;
- all prompts and the scoring rubric stay within the boundaries above.

Set `CONTENT_PACK_APPROVED=true` only after this review is documented.
