# ExitLoop: Constrained AI for Trait-Inheritance Explanations

*Seventh-Grade Biology Pilot · Georgia GSE S7L3.a*

## Draft ASEE Abstract

Middle-school biology teachers need timely, low-stakes evidence of students' understanding without losing instructional time. A conventional exit ticket can show an incomplete answer but may not reveal which scientific relationship is missing or incompatible. Catching those gaps matters because biology topics build on one another, and unresolved foundational ideas can make later concepts harder to understand.

This work presents ExitLoop, a teacher-governed formative-assessment prototype aligned to Georgia Standard of Excellence S7L3.a: construct an explanation supported with scientific evidence of the role of genes and chromosomes in inheriting a specific trait. During a 12–15 minute process, students explain a fictional beetle trait from a fixed evidence card, answer one follow-up question, revise their explanation, and apply the same relationships to a fictional plant trait.

For every student, the system checks the locked initial explanation against four target relationships and a fixed list of research-grounded possible alternative conceptions. It selects one prewritten question from a frozen bank; it does not generate questions, grade responses, or determine mastery. A short or unclear explanation receives a clarification question. If all four target relationships are present and no incompatible claim is identified, the student receives a completion-check prompt that acknowledges coverage without assigning a grade.

The planned future classroom pilot gives approximately 30 students in one seventh-grade biology class the same response-specific workflow. The primary exploratory outcome is blinded targeted repair: whether the revision corrects the specific missing or incompatible relationship identified in the initial explanation without introducing a new incompatible claim. A common unaided near-transfer explanation examines whether students use the relationships in a new context. Without a comparison group, this pilot can examine feasibility and immediate revision but cannot estimate the causal effect of adaptive follow-up.

The secondary teacher-utility outcome is whether the locked class summary supports a timely, interpretable next instructional action, measured through time-to-action, the recorded action, response-tag review, and brief usability ratings. Additional outcomes include near-transfer performance, classifier–human agreement, clarification rate, completion, response timing, and student-reported clarity and pressure. Effect estimates and exact confidence intervals will be treated as exploratory evidence of feasibility, targeted repair, measurement quality, and teacher decision support—not as proof of durable learning or general effectiveness.
