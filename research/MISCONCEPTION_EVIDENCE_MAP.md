# Trait-Inheritance Misconception Evidence Map

## Purpose and interpretation

These categories are observable response patterns used to choose a teacher-authored probe. They are not AI-generated diagnoses, stable traits of a learner, or research outcome scores. A relationship that is absent from a short answer is coded as missing evidence unless the student makes an explicit incompatible claim.

The map is bounded to Georgia GSE S7L3.a and the fictional single-gene evidence tasks. It does not require Punnett squares, probability calculations, memorized cell-division stages, or a model of complex human inheritance.

## Research basis

- Williams and colleagues studied 209 seventh-grade students and documented difficulty coordinating genes, chromosomes, genetic information, cell division, and contributions from two parents. Their results support explanation tasks that explicitly connect structural location and inheritance relationships. [Williams et al., *Science Education*](https://doi.org/10.1002/sce.20465)
- Lewis and Wood-Robinson reported persistent difficulty seeing relationships among genes, chromosomes, cell division, and inheritance. [Lewis & Wood-Robinson, *International Journal of Science Education*](https://doi.org/10.1080/095006900289949)
- Lewis and Kattmann analyzed how learners coordinate traits, genes, particles, and information, including conflation of genes with traits. [Lewis & Kattmann, *International Journal of Science Education*](https://doi.org/10.1080/0950069032000072782)
- The AAAS Project 2061 assessment collection, now hosted by BSCS Science Learning, publishes middle-grades genetics items and misconception statistics for ideas including genes as traits, chromosomes/genes lacking hereditary information, one-parent or same-sex-parent contribution, and acquired characteristics. [BSCS Science Assessment: DNA and inherited characteristics](https://assess.bscs.org/science/topics/1/RH/300) and [inheritance across generations](https://assess.bscs.org/science/topics/1/RH/302)
- The [Genetics Concept Assessment](https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.08-08-0045) supports concept-specific diagnostic items developed through expert review and student interviews. Its validation population was introductory college biology, so ExitLoop uses it as a design precedent rather than evidence that an item is valid for Grade 7.
- The [Conceptual Inventory of Natural Selection](https://doi.org/10.1002/tea.10053) supports the general method of mapping responses to documented alternative conceptions. ExitLoop uses only the inherited-versus-acquired distinction relevant to S7L3.a; natural-selection constructs are outside this pilot.

All ExitLoop prompts below are original. The cited sources ground the scientific relationships and response patterns; no copyrighted assessment item is copied.

## Routing map

| Pattern ID | Research-grounded pattern | Positive evidence required | Do not infer from | Teacher-authored probe | Two-minute teacher response |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `gene_is_the_trait` | Gene and visible trait are treated as the same entity | Explicitly states that the gene *is* curved bristles or that the visible body part itself was passed | Merely uses “gene for bristles” or omits a definition | Ask whether parents passed bristles or trait-related information and require evidence for each | Sort “gene versions” and “curved bristles” into inherited-information vs observed-trait columns; connect with an evidence arrow |
| `genes_lack_hereditary_information` | Genes do not contain inherited information | Explicitly says genes contain no hereditary information or only chromosomes contain it | Mentions chromosomes without defining genes | Ask what information the bristle-shape gene contains and how it relates to the trait | Mark the gene on a chromosome and label the trait-related information associated with that gene |
| `genes_and_chromosomes_unrelated` | Genes and chromosomes are separate or unrelated inheritance systems | Explicit denial that chromosomes carry genes, or an incompatible separate-path claim | Fails to mention chromosome in a short response | Ask where the evidence locates the gene and what structure carried each version | Mark one gene location on paired chromosome strips and trace one strip from each parent |
| `one_parent_determines_trait` | One parent, often the same-sex parent, supplies the relevant inherited information | Explicit “only mother/father” or same-sex-parent rule | Names only one parent while describing one piece of evidence | Ask which parent contributed each gene version in the offspring's pair | Color-code the two offspring chromosomes by parent and label the gene version on each |
| `parents_contribute_different_traits` | Each parent provides information for different traits | Explicitly assigns bristle shape to one parent and a different trait to the other | Discusses only one parental contribution without making a division-of-traits claim | For the same bristle-shape gene, ask what version came from each parent | Trace one version of the same gene from each parent into the offspring's chromosome pair |
| `acquired_trait_is_inherited` | A body change acquired during life changes what is inherited | Explicitly claims trimming or another acquired body change is passed to offspring | Notes that environment can influence some traits without claiming inheritance | Ask whether trimming changes the gene versions on chromosomes passed to offspring | Contrast a body-only change with a change to inherited chromosome information |

## Required relationship map

| Relationship ID | Minimum evidence in a response | Missing-evidence prompt |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `gene_trait_information` | Gene described as inherited information related to bristle shape, distinct from the visible bristle trait | Separate inherited information from what the offspring later grows |
| `gene_on_chromosome` | Gene/version explicitly located on or carried by chromosome 3 | Locate the gene and name the carrying structure |
| `both_parent_contributions` | One relevant chromosome or gene version traced from each parent | Identify the b version from each parent |
| `evidence_linked_explanation` | At least two evidence facts linked through because/therefore reasoning to curved bristles | Link the inherited b/b evidence with the b/b–curved evidence |

## Frozen student-facing question bank

| Prompt ID | Use when | Student-facing prompt | Source basis |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `inheritance_gene_trait_probe_01` | Gene and visible trait are conflated, or the gene–trait information relationship is missing | Did the parents pass curved bristles themselves, or information related to bristle shape? Name the gene evidence and explain what the offspring later grew. | AAAS/BSCS RH/300 |
| `inheritance_gene_information_probe_01` | Response explicitly denies that genes contain hereditary information | What information does the bristle-shape gene contain, and how is that information related to the offspring's bristle trait? Use one fact from the evidence card. | AAAS/BSCS RH/300; GCA design precedent |
| `inheritance_chromosome_probe_01` | Gene–chromosome relationship is incompatible or missing | Where does the evidence card locate the bristle-shape gene, and what structure carried each gene version from parent to offspring? Add that gene–chromosome relationship to your revision. | AAAS/BSCS RH/300 |
| `inheritance_both_parents_probe_01` | Response uses a one-parent or same-sex-parent rule, or omits the second contribution | The offspring has b and b. Which parent contributed each b version, and on what chromosome was each version carried? Cite evidence about both parents in your revision. | AAAS/BSCS RH/302 |
| `inheritance_same_trait_both_parents_probe_01` | Response says parents contribute different traits | For this one bristle-shape gene, what version came from each parent? Explain why both contributions are relevant to the offspring's bristle shape. | AAAS/BSCS RH/302 |
| `inheritance_acquired_trait_probe_01` | Response says a change acquired during life is inherited | If a parent beetle's bristles were trimmed, would that change the gene versions on the chromosomes passed to its offspring? Use the evidence card to explain what is inherited. | AAAS/BSCS RH/302; CINS method precedent |
| `inheritance_evidence_probe_01` | Scientific relationships are present but evidence is not linked to the conclusion | Choose two facts from the evidence card: one about what the offspring inherited and one about which gene-version pattern is associated with curved bristles. Explain how those facts support your conclusion. | Georgia S7L3.a; GCA design precedent |
| `inheritance_complete_check_01` | All four relationships are present and no incompatible claim is identified | Your explanation already includes the key scientific ideas for this activity. Reread it and make any change that would connect your evidence and conclusion more clearly. If you would not change anything, continue to the next example. | Georgia S7L3.a; GCA design precedent |
| `inheritance_clarify_01` | Response is too short, unclear, contradictory without a clear category, or outside scope | Identify the gene version carried on the chromosome from each parent. Then explain how the two inherited versions connect to the offspring's curved-bristle trait, using evidence from the card. | Georgia S7L3.a; AAAS/BSCS RH/300 and RH/302 |
| `control_reflection_01` | Reflection comparison condition | Read your explanation once. Make sure each scientific claim is supported by the evidence card and that you explain how information moved from both parents to the offspring. Revise one relationship so another seventh-grade student could follow your reasoning. | Georgia S7L3.a; GCA design precedent |

## Classifier decision policy

1. Classify only explicit evidence in the response.
2. Ignore spelling and grammar as evidence of science understanding.
3. Do not infer a misconception from omission alone.
4. Route explicit incompatible claims before missing relationships.
5. If fewer than seven words, low confidence, contradictory without a clear category, or outside scope, abstain and show `inheritance_clarify_01`.
6. Return only identifiers in the frozen schema; never generate feedback text.
7. Human raters independently code the research outcomes and classifier agreement.
8. When all four relationships are evidenced and there is no incompatible claim, use `inheritance_complete_check_01`. This acknowledges task coverage without assigning a grade or claiming mastery.

## Content-review questions

Before approval, the teacher/advisor should decide whether the initial evidence card makes the simplified B/b relationship sufficiently clear without accidentally assessing dominance vocabulary, whether “gene version” should be introduced alongside or instead of “allele,” and whether all wording is accessible to the participating class.
