# Cellular Respiration Misconception Evidence Map

**Status: research-derived draft; local teacher validation is required.**

The categories below are not AI-generated diagnoses and must not become permanent labels attached to students. They are observable response patterns derived from biology-education literature, bounded to the Georgia S7L4.b learning objective, and converted into teacher-reviewable routing rules.

## Evidence base

- Mohan, Chen, and Anderson developed a carbon-cycling learning progression using assessment and interview evidence from upper-elementary through high-school students. Their findings support eliciting whether students trace matter across organismal and ecosystem scales rather than treating food only as an enabler of actions. [Journal article](https://doi.org/10.1002/tea.20314)
- Hartley and colleagues documented the recurring pattern that students convert matter into energy, treat respiration as breathing, or fail to trace carbon through cellular respiration. Their diagnostic-question work supports separating matter and energy pathways. [Open paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3292066/)
- Parker and colleagues found that students can select correct answers while holding mixed ideas about photosynthesis, respiration, growth, matter, and energy. Their work supports combining a choice/classification with an explanation rather than relying on answer accuracy alone. [Open paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3292062/)
- Research with lower-secondary students documents confusion between photosynthesis and plant respiration, including the belief that plants do not respire or that photosynthesis is plant respiration. [ERIC record](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1020738)

Some cited validation samples are older or above seventh grade. These sources justify candidate constructs, not direct validity for this classroom. Cognitive interviews, teacher review, local pilot responses, and human-coding agreement are therefore mandatory.

## Frozen candidate categories

| ID | Observable response pattern | Include when | Do not infer when | Targeted probe | Two-minute teacher response |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `respiration_is_breathing_only` | Cellular respiration is identical to breathing or occurs only in lungs | The response explicitly defines respiration as breathing alone or omits cells/food/energy while making an exclusive breathing claim | The student merely mentions oxygen, breathing, or lungs | Contrast gas exchange with the cell process and ask for the connection | Place a lungs-level and cell-level diagram side by side; draw one connecting gas arrow |
| `matter_becomes_energy_or_disappears` | Food/carbon matter changes into energy or vanishes | The response explicitly converts matter to energy, says carbon disappears, or treats food matter as completely destroyed | The response says energy comes from food without claiming matter becomes energy | Ask the student to trace carbon atoms and energy as separate paths | Color-code carbon matter and energy arrows and require separate destinations |
| `plants_do_not_respire` | Only animals respire, or photosynthesis is the plant form of respiration | The response explicitly excludes plants from cellular respiration or equates the two processes | Plants are simply absent from an animal-focused answer | Ask what plant cells do with stored sugar when light is unavailable | Add cellular respiration to both plant and animal nodes in the class model |
| `energy_cycles_like_matter` | Energy returns through the ecosystem in the same loop as matter | The response uses a closed/repeating energy cycle or says energy is recycled back to producers | The student says energy moves between organisms without specifying a return loop | Ask which path cycles and which path eventually ends as heat | Draw circular matter arrows and a one-way energy arrow ending in heat |

## Priority routing

Only one targeted prompt is shown in the research condition. The frozen priority order is:

1. respiration-as-breathing-only;
2. matter-becomes-energy/disappears;
3. plants-do-not-respire;
4. energy-cycles-like-matter;
5. missing matter/energy relationship;
6. missing ecosystem connection;
7. clarification fallback when evidence is insufficient.

Priority is a product rule, not a claim that one idea is universally more important. The classroom teacher must approve this order before the content version is frozen.

## Validation plan

1. Ask the teacher to review the four categories against recent student work.
2. Conduct at least three cognitive interviews or think-aloud rehearsals with non-study students of similar age, if approved.
3. Build positive, negative, and ambiguous examples for every category.
4. Have two human coders label a pilot set while blind to AI output.
5. Revise category boundaries before—but never during—the comparison session.
6. Report AI precision, recall, F1, coverage, abstention, and the full confusion matrix by category.
